# Design

Hermex's look: **calm chat, colorful objects, one voice.** The reading surface stays quiet and neutral so long agent output is easy on the eyes. Color lives on discrete *things*: a tool step, a goal, a Brain page, an approval, a Brief card. Each kind of thing always wears the same color, rendered the same way everywhere (a glow), and every screen shares one type voice (SF Pro Rounded for display) and one header, so the Brief, Goals, and chat read as one app.

## Principles

1. **Neutral ground, colored objects.** Canvases, bubbles, and body text use the `hx*` neutrals. Color appears only on an object you could tap, link, or act on.
2. **A color means a kind.** Color is assigned by object type, never by sequence, position, or status. There are more kinds than hues, so some kinds share a paper (see Shared papers below), but one kind never wears two.
3. **One material: glow.** Every colored thing is its hue on a wash of itself, deep in light mode and bright in dark mode, at two strengths: a faint `wash` for small objects (chips, request cards, drawer and log icons) and an opaque medium `card` fill for whole cards (Brief cards, the Goal hero). There are no fixed light papers anymore.
4. **Glow text is full strength.** Text in a hue is never faded; hierarchy comes from size and weight. Fading is for decorative marks only (corner icons, dividers). Faded glow fails contrast.
5. **Nous blue means interactive.** `Color.accentColor` (`#0053FD` / `#4A84FE`) is the tint for controls, the send button, links, and "Atlas is working". It is never an object color. On a Daily Brief card's paper the card's ink is the tint (`.tint(palette.ink)`), so its controls, the dictation mic included, wear the card's color.
6. **Status colors stay semantic.** `hxSuccess`, `hxWarning`, and `hxDanger` mark outcomes (done, needs care, failed), and on Money a gain or a loss. They are not object colors.

### Papers (`HermexPaper`, `Config/HermexColors.swift`)

Each paper is one hue rendered as a glow that adapts to the appearance:

- `glow`: text and symbols in the hue (the "Glow" column below).
- `wash`: the hue at 16%, behind small objects.
- `card`: a whole card's opaque fill, the hue at 20% over `hxCanvas`. **Warm hues** (`opener`, `reading`, `past`, `plain`) go 12% over `hxSurface` in dark mode instead, because warm mixed into navy reads as muddy brown.
- `edge`: a 0.5 pt hairline in the hue, so a wash or card keeps its edge on the light canvas.
- `onGlow`: text on a solid `glow` fill (a primary button).
- `secondaryInk`: supporting text on a `card` or `wash`, never `hxTextSecondary`: the ink at 80% in light, the glow at 80% in dark (the light ink is dark-on-dark there).

`glow` and `secondaryInk` clear 4.5:1 on both `wash` and `card` in both appearances (`DesignSystemTests` checks every paper). `paper` and `ink` are the original light index-card values, kept as each hue's source.

### Shared papers

These papers carry more than one kind. Each group is related (a person, saved thinking, the past, Atlas's own machinery), so a shared hue still reads as kin. A new kind takes a free hue when one is left, else joins its closest kin here.

- `advisor`: the advisor's Check-in, Letter to her and letters in the Brain, a person (People pages, Catch up, From your people), memory and skill tool steps, Energy and mood.
- `book`: Book highlight, highlights in the Brain, and the Goals drawer row (a Book card never sits beside the drawer).
- `notes`: From your notes, Sleep, Lessons, file tool steps, the Feedback inbox, and the Daily Ask (its goal's paper).
- `video`: Video, web tool steps, Body.
- `past`: A year ago, Your journal, Looking back, Theme, the reviews' reflection questions.
- `plain`: From your library, Follow-ups, Atlas asks, What changed, Recap, shell and other tool steps.
- `opener`: requests waiting on you, the Brief opener and its dividers, People rows due for a catch-up.
- `dusk`: the weekly and monthly review frames, Looking ahead.

Add a new kind of object here and in the code together. A failed tool step keeps its color and adds an `hxDanger` mark; the color never turns red.

### Spending categories (`HermexSeries`, `Config/HermexColors.swift`)

Spending's charts color data, not objects, so the categories have their own set outside the papers: the share ring, the legend, and the dot on every category, charge, place and stand-out row. A category keeps its color in every month and in the year, and the ring keeps their order, so a slice never moves or repaints when the month changes. There is no blue, so a slice never reads as a control, and amounts beside a dot stay in `hxTextPrimary`. The order passed a colorblind check (adjacent pairs, the ring's wrap included) in both appearances. Groups without their own color (loans, fees and interest, fun, travel, cash, other) share `rest` and fold into one "Everything else" slice.

## Components

- **Card** (`HXCard`, `.hxCardSurface(_:)`, `HXCardRole` in `Config/HermexStyle.swift`): the one card. Its role decides the fill, edge, corners, and shadow: **glow** (one kind of thing: the paper's `card` fill and `edge`, `xl`, the `paper` shadow; Brief cards, review bites, timeline cards, Catch up, the Goal hero), **summary** (a neutral card that sits among glow cards: `hxSurface`, `hxSeparator` edge, `xl`, the `paper` shadow; the Brief opener, the review summary), **neutral** (`hxSurface`, `hxSeparator` edge, `lg`, flat; the same as `.hxCard()`), and **wash** (the paper's wash over `hxSurface` through `glowSurface`, `xl`, the `paper` shadow; Prep's run and home cards, whose controls take the paper's tint). `HXCard` stacks an optional kicker, a headline in `AppFont.display` semibold (in the glow on a colored card, `hxTextPrimary` on a neutral one, marked as a header), the body, and an optional footer, padded 16 pt; text keeps the colors its caller gives it. Cards whose words scroll inside them (Daily Deck cards, `BrainDeckCard`) use `.hxCardSurface(role, clipsContent: true)`, and a stack's bare papers fill `role.shape` with `role.fill`. Build new cards from these, never from a hand-rolled fill, stroke, and shadow. `scripts/check-design-drift` warns about fixed font sizes, numeric corner radii, raw materials, raw colors, and raw animation curves outside `Config/` (a documented exception ends its line with `// design-drift: allow`).
- **`HXChip`** (`Config/HermexStyle.swift`): the one chip. A capsule with the wash, edge, and glow, an optional symbol, a label (caption medium, one line, middle-truncated), and an optional trailing mark. Tool calls, run counts, Brain citations, and `+N` all use it. Lay chips out with `FlowRow`, which wraps and offers an over-wide chip the row's width so large text truncates instead of overflowing.
- **Live run.** While Atlas works, each call is a `ToolCallChip`: its target (cut at 24 characters), an ellipsis while running, a red `xmark` after failing. The newest four show; older calls sit behind `+N`.
- **Finished run.** One chip per kind of tool with its count, in the order each kind first ran ("3 commands", "2 searches"), plus a red `xmark` on a kind with failures. Tapping the row opens the full log; "Expand Tools by Default" starts it open. The row is at least 44 pt tall.
- **Tool log rows.** Each row's icon sits on a 20 pt tile in its kind's wash and glow, so the log matches the chip that opened it.
- **Brain connections** (`BrainConnectionsView.swift`). A page opens on its title, then the line under it ("Concept · updated 3 wk ago" for a wiki page, whose date the server labels; "Article · Oct 6, 2026" otherwise), then its tags at meta size: on a wiki or article page each "#tag" is a Nous-blue link to that list filtered to it (still not a chip; the list's chip bar opens with it selected and "All" to clear it), elsewhere one line of text. Pages have no cover: the first screen is the page. A page with links shows them as a sideways row of linked-page cards (`wash` glow in each kind's paper, `md` corners) under the title. Above the title, every page has a Nous-blue trail: "Brain", which goes to the Brain home from any page (popping back to it, or replacing the Brain pages on top when the page was opened from chat or the Daily Brief), then the parent hub when there is one. Linked and unlinked mentions are rows on a neutral card: a kind dot, the source title, and the words around the mention with the mention marked in the page's wash and glow. A hub says so under its title, and that line is a Nous-blue link with a down arrow that scrolls to its pages ("Hub · 8 pages · updated 3 wk ago"; a hub inside another hub says "8 pages under this one", so its count isn't read as the pager's) and lists its pages, in pager order, as **In this hub** ("Pages under this one" when the hub sits in another hub) after its own words and mentions, three rows with their gists then "Show all 8"; its link cards leave those pages out, and each opens inside that hub. A page in a hub drops the hub from its link cards (the trail names it) and gets a pager capsule at the foot, opaque `hxSurface` under the glass so text never shows through, growing with Dynamic Type (the hub's name wraps to two lines at accessibility sizes, as the trail's does); a step in flight shows a spinner in its arrow, and a failed one turns "4 of 14" into "Couldn't open it · tap to retry" in `hxWarning`, and tapping it retries that step (previous, the hub and "4 of 14", next; tapping the middle lists the hub's pages in a sheet, the current one checked); the next page slides in only once it has loaded. There is no sideways swipe on the body: code blocks and tables scroll sideways there. After the mentions, Further reading is two sections: **Sources** (each with its gist) and **Also tagged** (pages sharing at least two of the page's tags, each naming them, "#rome · #person"), at most three each.
- **People list** (`BrainPeopleList.swift`). Above Everyone, in order and each hidden when empty: **Due for a catch-up**, **Birthdays soon** (next 14 days) and **Recently seen** (last 30 days), under `BrainSectionHeader`s, in the server's order. Rows are `BrainRow`s: monogram, name, the newest encounter's headline (else the relationship) on one line, and a trailing time in `meta` ("6d", "3 wk", "4 mo", "2 yr"; birthdays "today", "tomorrow", "in 11d"), in the `opener` glow on due rows. A due row's subtitle leads with its rhythm ("Every 3 wk", from the server's `cadence_days`), or **Lapsed** once they are three rhythms past it, then why now: the first open thread of their current Catch up (`hook`), else the newest note ("Lapsed · Her new job"). VoiceOver reads "Name, hook, seen 4 months ago, due for a catch-up, lapsed, usually every 3 months". Headlines drop the invisible U+FFFC placeholder iOS text input leaves; a person page never shows a note's `_Source:` line. A search hides the sections. **Everyone** keeps today's rows, with a count and a relationship menu (Nous blue, "All" plus each `relationship_type` with its count) at the header's trailing edge.
- **Person page** (`BrainPersonTimelineView.swift`, when the server sends `person`). Under the header (`relationship · location · seen 6 days ago`): the **rhythm strip**, twelve 8 pt dots (scaled) for the last twelve months, oldest first, filled in the person glow for a month they were seen and `hxSeparator` otherwise, with the first and last month in `meta` at the ends (VoiceOver: "Seen in N of the last 12 months"; hidden unless the server sends twelve months); then at most three `HXChip`s in a `FlowRow` (birthday, company, else industry when there is no company or it runs over 32 characters, school; a value over 32 characters stays in About). Then **Catch up**, a glow card in the person paper (the Brief card treatment: `card` fill, `edge`, `xl`, `paper` shadow): "Catch up" in `AppFont.display` semibold in the glow, the nightly recap in `hxTextPrimary` body, then "· " lines in the glow, a birthday within 30 days first, then the open threads; hidden when there is neither recap nor line. Then **Timeline** (`HXSectionHeader`, the count trailing once there are more than three) over one neutral card (`.hxCard()`) of rows, newest first: the kind's tile (`BrainModuleIcon`: a note `person.2.fill` and a text `message.fill` in the person paper, a journal day `book.closed.fill` in the journal paper), the headline on up to two lines (a text shows its quoted message; a journal day's words in serif with the name marked), and when ("Oct 8 · 6 days ago") in `meta`, hairlines between. Three show, then "Show all N" in Nous blue. A tap opens the entry's card full screen (`opensAsFullCard`, the review's open card): kicker "Note · Oct 8 · 6 days ago" (or "Texted ·", "Journal ·") in the glow, the headline as the lead, its bullets and prose, a same-day journal day folded in as one serif caption line ("Journal: …"), and "Open the journal day" at the foot when there is one. ## Shape

One radius scale, as `HXRadius`:

| Token | pt | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `xs` | 6 | Inline code, thumbnails, log icon tiles |
| `sm` | 10 | Rows, attachment tiles, buttons, drawer icon tiles |
| `md` | 14 | Inset blocks, choice buttons |
| `lg` | 18 | Section cards, action cards (approval overlay, Bot request card) |
| `xl` | 28 | Glow cards (Brief cards, Goal hero) |

Use `.continuous` corners everywhere. Chips are capsules. The composer keeps its own geometry (`ChatComposerMetrics`: a pill, and 26 pt when expanded), and the clarification card that docks on it uses the same 26 so the two read as one surface.

## Elevation

Shadows, as `.hxShadow(_:)`:

- **`paper`**: black at 0.08, radius 14, y 6, for glow cards. **`stacked`** (0.06, 10, y 4) for deck cards waiting behind the top one.
- **`float`**: black at 0.12 (light) or 0.28 (dark), radius 14, y 6, for the composer and floating glass controls.
- **`modal`**: black at 0.22, radius 18, y 12, for a card over a dimmed screen (the approval overlay).

Everything inline in chat is flat.

## Type

One voice, three faces, all Dynamic Type through `AppFont`:

- **Display: SF Pro Rounded** (`AppFont.display`): screen titles, card headlines, Brief questions, Goal titles, and headings in Atlas's replies (the Markdown theme's headings use `FontFamily(.system(.rounded))`). Semibold.
- **Body: SF Pro**: everything you read at length, controls, metadata.
- **Saved words: New York** (`AppFont.serif`), only for words a person saved: quotes, book highlights, past journal entries, and recap answers. Prep's story scripts (behind "Show script" on a story rep, and the sample telling after it) and a boss's sample answer count, because they are built from Andrew's own stories; a lesson question's worked answer is not, so it stays in the body font. Nothing else is serif.

No `.system(size:)` on text. The one exception: an SF Symbol inside a fixed-size control (a round button, a media control) keeps a fixed point size, since a growing glyph would break the control.

## Motion

All motion comes from `HXMotion` (`Config/HermexMotion.swift`). Choose a curve by what is moving, never by screen, so a section opening in the Brief moves like one in chat. No view builds its own `.spring`, `.easeOut`, or bare `withAnimation {}`.

| Curve | Timing | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `press` | smooth, caller's duration | A control pressed down and released |
| `quickState` | easeInOut 0.16 s | A small state flip: a pick, a rating, a toggle, a hint or control chrome showing or hiding |
| `disclosure` | smooth 0.18 s | Expanding or collapsing in place: show more, a peek, a section or a worked answer opening |
| `chrome` | smooth 0.22 s | The composer's and session search's chrome growing and shrinking |
| `scroll` | easeOut 0.2 s | Scrolling to a row in code: the latest message, last night, the current exercise |
| `swap` | smooth 0.25 s | Content replaced in place: the next page, a new question, a refreshed chart, list rows added or removed |
| `panel` | smooth 0.3 s | A surface arriving or leaving: the drawer, a toast, an opened card, a picker over the composer |
| `card` | spring 0.4 s, bounce 0.15 | A deck card moving: swiped, flown off, pulled back, settled, fanned, dropped |
| `reveal` | easeOut 0.35 s | A row or tile arriving as its card comes on top; lists stagger by `rowStagger` (0.08 s) |
| `pop` | spring 0.4 s, bounce 0.3 | A mark landing: a dot filling (staggered by `dotStagger`, 0.025 s), a star, a seal, a bot's reaction |
| `draw` | easeInOut 1.2 s | A number counting up or a ring drawing itself, once |

`clarificationToggle` (easeOut 0.22 s, the keyboard's clock) and the transcript's row transitions stay chat-only.

**Reduce Motion.** Every curve takes `reduceMotion` and returns `nil`, so the change snaps, never a shorter ease. The one exception is `panel`, which returns `HXMotion.crossfade` (easeInOut 0.2 s), because a surface appearing must still read as a change. The deck uses the same crossfade to turn a page instead of flying a card, and its fan opens and closes without moving. Movement transitions (`bottomOverlayTransition`, `disclosureTransition`) become a plain fade.

A live chip fades in as its call arrives (off for replays and under Reduce Motion), and opening the log uses `disclosure`. A card or chip that opens a screen is its zoom source (`.zoomSource(for:cornerRadius:)`): the screen grows out of it and shrinks back into it on the way back, with the system's crossfade under Reduce Motion. Goal cards, Brain home rows, link cards, module grid cards, and chat citation chips zoom; screens the drawer opens slide in as usual.

**Nothing loops or repaints continuously**, with four exceptions, each still under Reduce Motion:

- **Prep's sparkle.** While Atlas grades a Prep answer, the submit button's sparkle breathes (a symbol effect) beside a once-a-second counter, because that wait runs long enough to look stuck. The Prep tutor's "Thinking" bubble uses the same sparkle until its first words stream in.
- **Prep's story clock.** While a story is told, its clock label (only the label) ticks once a second, because the telling is timed against 4:00 like the interview. It stops when the story is sent.
- **The call's constellation** (`VoiceCallStarfield`, `VoiceCallMind.swift`) is a mind of stars and redraws at 60 fps while Atlas thinks and speaks and 30 fps while idle or listening, because it follows the live audio levels. About 2,000 stars make a 3D cloud with no drawn outline (knots around each call memory, threads along the links between them, a soft halo), each joined to its nearest neighbors by faint bowed fibers (`MindNetwork`), inside a geodesic membrane of 220 points (`MindLattice`) that is brighter toward the limb; a slow noise field drifts the stars and the cloud turns in 3D with perspective. The ball never breaks. Listening cools it; the membrane trembles with the mic and each word heard closes in from the surface as a shell of light. Thinking sends six seekers (`MindSeekers`) walking the network a fiber at a time toward places they haven't been, lighting what they visit, with sparks running faster along the fibers. Each memory Atlas finds pulls signals in through the network until it ignites in its paper with its name beside it, the membrane near it takes its color, and a route along the fibers grows from the previous memory to it. When the reply starts, a pulse runs that chain in 0.8 s and the network rings out from it once, with a soft bloom. Speaking warms it: the memory Atlas speaks from breathes with its voice (loudness smoothed over about 0.4 s), and each phrase (at most one every 1.1 s, `VoicePulses`) spreads brightly hop by hop from that memory across most of the mind, moving to the next memory after a pause or every third phrase; the swell is per phrase, never per word. Muted while it's the user's turn, or reconnecting, the whole mind dims to about half. Cutting Atlas off snaps toward listening on the next frame with a light haptic. Each call lays out a fresh mind, and a memory used again later in the call lights in the same place (`ConstellationLayout.slot`); each new shape is wired off the main thread. Star and fiber colors are raw per-frame components inside the Canvas (`StarSprites`, marked `design-drift: allow`), not tokens; memory nodes wear their papers' dark glows. Reduce Motion pauses the timeline and draws each phase still, crossfading between phases. Measured on the iPhone 17 simulator in a Debug build: about 3.5 ms to step and record a frame, under 5 ms at worst.
- **The bot face** (`BotFaceMotion`) plays short scripted bits (a glance, a blink, a hop; 0.5–1.6 s each) at 60 fps, idle ones every 4–9 s while the face is on screen, so a bot reads as present. Between bits nothing redraws. Reduce Motion skips the bits and holds the face still.
